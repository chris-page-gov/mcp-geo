# Arden / Henley Belt — Priority 3 Assessment
## BROCKHURST Series / B4089, B4095, Rural Network

> [!info] Priority 3 — High (5-year planning horizon)
> **Arden / Henley belt, central-west Warwickshire**
> Dominant series: BROCKHURST 1 (13.91%) + BROCKHURST 2 (12.18%) + OAK 1 (10.44%) — combined 36.5%
> Primary hazard: **Shrink-swell moderate-high** (impeded Triassic/glacial clay); severe waterlogging
> Network at risk: B4089, B4095, rural unclassified network; Forest of Arden lanes
> Recommended actions: Monitor; integrate into 5-year maintenance planning

---

## 1. Zone Delineation

The Arden zone covers the historic Forest of Arden in central-west Warwickshire:

- **North**: Solihull / Birmingham fringe (52.45°N)
- **South**: Stratford-upon-Avon / Avon valley (52.20°N)
- **West**: Redditch / Worcestershire border (~-2.00°W)
- **East**: Fosse Way / Leamington corridor (~-1.60°W)

Key settlements: Henley-in-Arden, Lapworth, Wootton Wawen, Claverdon, Tanworth-in-Arden, Redditch (fringe), Studley, Knowle.

The zone is geologically distinct from both the Feldon (Jurassic Lias clay) and North Warwickshire (Triassic Mercia Mudstone outcrop). The Arden BROCKHURST and OAK series develop on **Triassic Mercia Mudstone overlain by glacial till** — the clay is of mixed glacial and in-situ origin, producing a heterogeneous but consistently clay-rich subsoil.

**Assessment bbox**: `[-2.00, 52.20, -1.60, 52.45]`

## 3. NATMAP Point — Lapworth Area (OAK 1)

*Source: `landis_natmap_point(52.35°N, -1.73°W)`, Lapworth/Henley-in-Arden area*

- **Series confirmed**: OAK 1
- **Description**: "Seasonally wet deep red loam to clay"
- **Geology**: "Reddish till" (glacial origin — Wolstonian ice sheet deposits)
- **Dominant soils**: "Slowly permeable seasonally waterlogged fine loamy over clayey soils"
- **Land use**: "Winter cereals and some short term grassland, stock rearing and dairying"
- **Drainage**: Impeded
- **Water protection**: *"Main risks are associated with overland flow from compacted or poached fields. Organic slurry, dirty water, fertiliser, pathogens and fine sediment can all move in suspension or solution with overland flow or drain water"*

The OAK 1 description confirms the glacial origin of the clay — red Wolstonian till deposited over Triassic mudstone. This produces a soil that is **impeded** (not just slightly impeded like WHIMPLE) with a pronounced fine-loamy surface over a plastic clay subsoil. The "fine loamy over clayey" texture description is key: the surface horizon is looser and more workable than the clay subsoil, but the transition is abrupt, concentrating water at the clay interface — exactly the zone where road subgrade problems originate.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ap | 0–15 | Clay loam | 26 | 35 | Mod. coarse subangular | **Common (10YR5/2)** | Mottles at surface — extreme waterlogging |
| ABw | 15–30 | Clay loam | 23 | 35 | Weak medium subangular | **Common (10YR5/4)** | Slightly porous; quartzite stones |
| Bw | 30–45 | Clay loam | 24 | 32 | Mod. medium subangular | **Many (75YR5/2 + 75YR5/8)** | Fe-Mn nodules; bi-coloured mottling |
| Bg | 45–65 | Clay | 48 | 36 | Mod. fine angular blocky | **Many (5GY6/1)** + few (10YR5/6) | Greenish-grey reducing horizon |
| Cg | 65–115 | Clay | 46 | 32 | **Massive** | **Common (N5)** | Neutral grey; slightly calcareous |

**Topsoil chemistry**: pH 6.0, CARBON 3.4%; quartzite stones throughout; Cu-EDTA 28.7, Pb-EDTA 67.3 (elevated)

**Key diagnostic features — engineering interpretation:**

1. **Mottles from surface (0cm)** — common grey mottles in the topsoil (10YR5/2 = greyish brown) indicate that the water table rises to the surface during wet winters. This is the most extreme waterlogging found in any Warwickshire series profiled in this assessment. The road subgrade is saturated for extended periods each winter.

2. **5GY6/1 (greenish-grey) at 45–65cm** — a strongly gleyed horizon with greenish colouring indicates **reducing conditions** (ferrous iron; anaerobic). This is a Bg (gleyed B) horizon that is anaerobic for months each year. Water trapped at this depth cannot drain naturally.

3. **N5 (neutral grey Munsell) at 65–115cm** — fully reduced subsoil; essentially saturated and anaerobic permanently at depth. The C horizon is massive (no structure) — parent Triassic mudstone.

4. **Clay 48% at peak (45–65cm)** — above the 35% vertic threshold; moderate shrink-swell potential. Below the 54% of EVESHAM (Feldon) but substantially above the minimum for meaningful seasonal movement.

5. **No strongly developed angular blocky vertic structure** — the fine angular blocky at 45–65cm is weaker than EVESHAM's strongly developed structure. Shrink-swell is real but at moderate intensity.

6. **Elevated lead (Pb-EDTA 67.3)** in topsoil — possibly reflecting atmospheric deposition, historical orchard applications, or proximity to historical industrial activity in the Redditch/Birmingham fringe. Not directly relevant to road infrastructure but notable for any disturbed ground.

> [!warning] Critical finding
> BROCKHURST is characterised by **waterlogging as the primary engineering hazard**, not shrink-swell alone. Mottles at surface level mean the road subgrade is in a saturated state for substantial periods of the year. Roads on BROCKHURST clay that lack adequate drainage are carrying traffic on effectively a saturated clay subgrade in winter — load-bearing capacity is severely reduced under these conditions.

**~87% clay or clay-loam subsoil** — the highest proportion of any zone analysed in this assessment series.

This counter-intuitive result (the Arden, classified as Priority 3, has a higher subsoil clay proportion than the Priority 1 Feldon at ~82%) requires explanation. The BROCKHURST + OAK 1 + SALOP combination in the Arden produces a remarkably clay-dominant subsoil texture profile. However, the key distinction from the Feldon is that the Arden clays:
- Have **lower peak plasticity** (no pure vertic Lias clay; BROCKHURST peaks at 48% not 54%)
- **Lack the NATMAP landslip flag** (no documented slope failure mechanism)
- Have **lower shrink-swell intensity** despite high clay content (Triassic/glacial clay minerals have lower swelling capacity than Jurassic Lias smectite)
- Occur on **flatter terrain** (Forest of Arden is a plateau, not a dissected clay escarpment)

The 87% clay/clay-loam subsoil figure means that waterlogging is essentially ubiquitous across the zone — virtually no road in the Arden network crosses purely well-drained ground.

## 7. Risk Characterisation

### 7.1 Primary mechanism: chronic waterlogging-driven subgrade deterioration

The dominant failure mode on BROCKHURST roads is not acute (landslip, rapid pavement failure) but **chronic and cumulative**:

1. **Winter saturation** — water table rises to surface level; road subgrade clay becomes saturated; bearing capacity drops significantly
2. **Traffic loading on saturated clay** — pore water pressure under wheel loads cannot dissipate through impermeable clay; clay pumps laterally under load; pavement cracking initiates
3. **Spring recovery incomplete** — clay drains slowly; some seasons the water table never fully drops before autumn recharge; subgrade never reaches design strength
4. **Progressive fatigue** — each seasonal cycle degrades pavement integrity; asphalt cracks widen; water infiltration through cracks accelerates the next cycle
5. **Road profile degradation** — rutting and transverse cracking in summer; edge-break and pothole formation in late autumn/winter

This pattern is slow (years to decades) rather than fast, which is why the 5-year planning horizon is appropriate. The road does not fail suddenly; it gradually becomes uneconomical to maintain at current reactive spend levels.

### 7.2 Moderate shrink-swell overlay

The BROCKHURST 48% clay peak means moderate shrink-swell is present alongside the waterlogging mechanism:
- Summer: clay subgrade dries and shrinks; surface cracking; transverse cracking in road edges
- Autumn: rapid recharge into cracks; clay swells; heave
- This is a milder version of the Feldon mechanism — same process, lower amplitude

In the Arden, **the seasonal cycle is modulated by tree cover** — the Forest of Arden retains significant mature woodland (oak, ash, hazel). Tree roots extract soil moisture preferentially in summer, intensifying desiccation beneath and adjacent to roads; root channels also preferentially drain clay in winter. Roads through or adjacent to woodland on BROCKHURST clay should be treated as higher-risk within the zone.

## 8. Recommended Actions

### 8.1 Monitor — Entry into 5-Year Maintenance Planning ✅ METHOD DEFINED

**Status: Monitoring framework defined. Integration into routine PMS review required.**

The priority-3 designation means the zone does not require the immediate intervention programme of Priority 1 (Feldon). The appropriate action is systematic monitoring that triggers intervention when condition thresholds are crossed.

**Monitoring indicators for BROCKHURST roads:**
- **Primary indicator**: longitudinal cracking parallel to road edge — indicates clay subgrade movement/waterlogging at road margin
- **Secondary indicator**: transverse cracking at regular intervals — indicates shrink-swell cycling fatigue
- **Tertiary indicator**: rutting in wheel tracks — indicates clay pumping under traffic (subgrade saturation during winter loading)
- **Trigger level**: SCANNER or SCRIM survey score falling below threshold → flag for drainage inspection before repair

**Integration into 5-year maintenance cycle:**

| Year | Action |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | NATMAP BROCKHURST/OAK 1 polygons clipped to road network (USRN join); BROCKHURST road register created |
| Year 1–2 | PMS cross-reference: reactive spend per USRN on BROCKHURST vs non-clay roads; identify top-quartile spend roads as early intervention candidates |
| Year 2–3 | Drainage inspection of top-quartile spend roads on BROCKHURST; ditch-clearing; gully survey |
| Year 3–4 | Surface condition survey (SCANNER/visual) of full BROCKHURST road register; update risk scores |
| Year 5 | Review and update: escalate roads meeting intervention criteria to Priority 2 action track; adjust monitoring frequency based on observed deterioration rates |

### 8.2 Design standard for future works on BROCKHURST

When BROCKHURST or OAK 1 roads reach scheduled resurfacing:
- **Specify drainage first**: ensure edge drains are clear and functional before laying new surface
- **Sub-base standard**: minimum 150mm granular sub-base where BROCKHURST clay is within 300mm of formation level
- **Cross-fall**: minimum 2.5% — clay roads lose cross-fall through repeated patching; inadequate cross-fall accelerates waterlogging
- **Woodland proximity**: where road is adjacent to mature woodland on BROCKHURST, consider root barrier installation at edge of carriageway to manage differential moisture extraction

### 8.3 Note on Arden/Feldon boundary roads

The southern margin of the Arden bbox intersects the northern margin of the Feldon zone. Roads crossing the BROCKHURST/DENCHWORTH or BROCKHURST/EVESHAM 2 boundary (notably B4095 approaching the Fosse Way from Redditch) may carry **compound risk** — waterlogging from BROCKHURST on the upper section, transitioning to shrink-swell + landslip from DENCHWORTH on the lower section. These transition-zone roads should be flagged in PMS and given a higher monitoring frequency than standard BROCKHURST roads.

## 9. Three-Zone Comparison

| Parameter | Feldon (P1) | N. Warwickshire (P2) | Arden (P3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parent geology | Jurassic Lias clay | Triassic Mercia Mudstone | Triassic/glacial till |
| Primary series | DENCHWORTH/EVESHAM | WHIMPLE 2/3 | BROCKHURST 1/2 + OAK 1 |
| Peak NSI clay % | 54% | 43% | 48% |
| Vertic structure | Strongly developed | Moderately developed | Moderately developed |
| Gley onset depth | 24cm | 10cm | **0cm (surface)** |
| Shrink-swell intensity | HIGH | MODERATE | MODERATE-HIGH |
| Landslip flag | YES | No | No |
| BGS landslide records | 58 | 6 | 5 |
| Subsoil clay/clay-loam % | ~82% | ~69% | **~87%** |
| Primary risk | Shrink-swell + landslip | Waterlogging + moderate shrink-swell | **Chronic waterlogging** + moderate shrink-swell |
| Priority horizon | Immediate | 12 months | 5 years |

The Arden's 87% clay/clay-loam subsoil — the highest of the three zones — combined with gley from the surface explains why "moderate-high" is the correct risk rating despite lower landslip potential. The clay is pervasive and persistently wet. The absence of acute failure modes (landslip, strong vertic structure) justifies Priority 3 rather than Priority 1 or 2, but the chronic drainage-driven deterioration will accumulate costs steadily if roads are not managed proactively.
