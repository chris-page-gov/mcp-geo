# Building Climate-Resilient Roads in Lincolnshire

![[Building Climate-Resilient Roads.png]]

**Full title:** *Crumbling Foundations: Building Climate-Resilient Roads in Lincolnshire*

> [!abstract] This infographic distils the Lincolnshire case study into its two essential halves: the perfect storm of vulnerability (why roads fail) and the engineering blueprint for resilience (what to do about it). It is the sharpest single-page summary of the problem and solution set. See the full 10-slide strategic document at [[Subsurface Road Resilience Blueprint]].

---

## Left Panel — The Perfect Storm of Vulnerability

### The Headline Numbers

**87%** of the network consists of "evolved" roads — originated as mud tracks and lack engineered foundations. Without a sub-base, the road surface sits directly on the compressible soil.

**Compressibility is the primary driver of failure.** Peat and tidal flat deposits are highly susceptible to compression under vehicle loading. This is the finding that reframes the entire maintenance conversation: the problem is underground, not on the surface.

**Deterioration is 3× faster on high-risk ground.** Roads on "Very Significant" compressible ground (GeoSure class E) see much higher annual residual life loss.

### Annual Deterioration by GeoSure Compressibility Rating

| BGS GeoSure Class | Rating | Years of Residual Life Lost Per Year |
|---|---|---|
| A | No indicator (Low) | 0.83 |
| D | Significant | 1.13 |
| E | Very Significant | **3.10** |

> [!warning] The multiplier
> A road on GeoSure class E compressible ground loses residual life **3.7× faster** than one on class A. On evolved roads without foundations, the full 10-slide data shows this can reach 3–17 years of structural life lost per year (see [[Subsurface Road Resilience Blueprint]] Slide 7).

## Right Panel — Engineering for a Resilient Future

### 1. Transitioning from "Patching" to "Recycling"

The core engineering shift. Instead of adding more asphalt on top of a failing surface (which adds load, compresses the sub-base further, and accelerates the failure cycle), **recycling** reuses existing road material to reduce added weight and prevent further subsoil compression.

> [!important] Why patching accelerates failure
> Patching adds 40–80mm of dense tarmac — a significant static load on already-compressed peat or tidal flat deposits. This is the same mechanism the BGS/LCC study identified: the repair is literally making the underlying problem worse.

**Recycling breaks this cycle** by working with the same material mass rather than adding to it.

### 2. The Fodderdyke Bank Reinforcement

Site 1 from the BGS case study — 5–10m peat depth, C-class road, scene of a 2015 fatality. The engineering response: **steel mesh installation** to help the road move as a single entity rather than fracturing along differential settlement lines.

The mesh distributes load across the peat surface, preventing localised shear failures and the severe undulations documented at the site. It does not prevent settlement but it prevents catastrophic surface rupture.

### 3. Climate-Informed Maintenance Prioritisation

Using **BGS GeoClimate datasets** to predict soil moisture deficits and target high-risk zones early — before visible failure occurs. Combined with **soil moisture sensors** installed in the road verge to monitor desiccation in real time.

This is the operational implementation of the BGS GeoSure + UKCP18 screening approach: not just a one-off assessment but a continuous monitoring and prioritisation system.

## Key Concepts Summarised

| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| Evolved road | Road originated as a historical trackway, widened by successive patching, with no engineered foundation |
| Compressibility as primary driver | The failure mechanism is subsurface compression, not surface wear |
| Patching → acceleration cycle | Each patch adds load → compresses susceptible soil → accelerates future failure |
| Recycling | Reuse existing asphalt material to avoid adding load — the correct intervention on compressible ground |
| GeoClimate prioritisation | Using BGS GeoSure + UKCP18 combined product to target maintenance before failure |
| Soil moisture sensors | In-situ monitoring to detect desiccation events early |
